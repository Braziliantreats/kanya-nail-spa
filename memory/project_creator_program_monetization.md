## 1. What this project is

MayhemTBD's creator program gives creators a **free, hosted, brand-ready media-kit site** — not a template they download, a live page we build and hand them a link to. Free for 6 months, then it converts to a paid subscription to keep it live.

- **Template Kit** — 3 layouts, preset palettes. Free 6 months → $150/mo.
- **Custom Kit** — 6 layouts, custom fonts, niche graphics. Free 6 months → $300/mo.
- **The Block** — 9 cultural themes exclusively for Black creators, available on any tier. The community moat.

Qualifying costs attention, not money: apply + follow @MayhemTBD + tag us in a story + pin a post with the kit link (Custom adds: follow on 2+ platforms + refer 2 creators). Those requirements ARE the acquisition engine — every creator becomes a billboard and a referrer.

**Core philosophy (unchanged):** never pitch. The kit sells upgrades itself (✦ add-on tags on premium features), brands seeing pro kits create demand-pull, money is never the first conversation, automation is never revealed. Value first; the ask is always soft.

## 2. The play (agreed direction, after research)

The kit is the same product as our specialized pages for cleaners and car detailers: **a page whose only job is to make the person with the money say yes.** For creators, the person with the money is a brand. Everything follows from that.

1. **Fix the front door first.** mayhemtbd.com/creatorprogram currently shows the homepage — nobody can apply. Nothing launches until this URL works. (Research "Finding Zero.")
2. **Build kits that impress the brand, not just the creator.** Every kit leads with what brands actually check: who the audience is (one specific sentence), results from past collabs as structured "case cards" (reach, engagement vs. normal range, code redemptions), fresh stats with dates, and a clear offer/terms menu. New creator with zero deals? A wizard builds their first case card from their best organic post so the kit never looks empty.
3. **Sell fewer, better boosters — the ones that get creators booked and paid.** Drop the decoration boosters. The new lineup, in order: results/case-study builder with after-campaign reports (the star — wins re-bookings), rates + usage-rights menu (captures the ~40% extra brands pay to reuse content in ads), fast booking + clear terms wrapper, "who viewed your kit" analytics. The ✦ quiet-sell mechanic stays exactly as designed — it just sells these instead.
4. **Keep the fun stuff, move it down the page.** Fan wall, Top-8, cultural themes stay — they're the growth engine. But brands skim for 5–10 seconds on a phone, so results come first, personality after. Never between the brand and the proof.
5. **Use the free 6 months to stack wins, then charge with receipts.** Help creators pitch, track kit views and brand inquiries from day one, and at month 6 convert with evidence: "Your kit got 214 views and 9 brand inquiries — keep it live for $150/mo." A kit that visibly worked converts; a kit that sat there won't.

**Homework before promising volume:** hand-build ~10 real kits and time them. That number (minutes of human work per kit) is the real cost of every free kit and decides how many we can give away.

## 3. The original 5-layer monetization plan (pre-research reference)

- **Layer 1 — Trial-to-subscription flip:** 6 months free → $150/$300 per month to keep the kit live. Backbone recurring revenue.
- **Layer 2 — Boosters à la carte:** one-time add-ons sold silently by the kit (original menu: showreel/testimonials/press page $50–100; SEO/live feed/gallery $150–200; booking/rate calculator/PDF/fan wall $100–350; analytics/heatmap/Wrapped/seasonal refresh $150–400 per year).
- **Layer 3 — Full paid builds:** Starter $600, Glow Up $1,800, The Brand $2,400+.
- **Layer 4 — Transaction cut:** 1–2% via Stripe Connect when brands book through the kit.
- **Layer 5 — The Block + niche communities:** lowers churn, drives referrals, makes layers 1–4 durable.
- **Growth loops:** pinned post, story tags, refer-2-to-qualify, collab circles, The Block word-of-mouth.
- **Unit-economics assumption:** each free kit costs near-zero to host; money is in retention + stacked boosters + community.

## 4. What the research found — and how it changes the plan

The research (9 files, appended below) studied how creator–brand deals actually work, on both sides, with every claim graded by evidence quality. Verdict: **the philosophy survives; the menu doesn't.** Specifics:

**4.0 BLOCKER.** mayhemtbd.com/creatorprogram serves the homepage, verified twice. Fix before anything ships or gets pitched.

**4.1 The month-6 flip is conditional, not automatic.** The kit converts brand interest but does not create it (~75% of cold pitch emails are never opened), and our likeliest signups are new creators with zero proof. If 6 months pass and the kit didn't visibly land deals, "pay to keep it" collapses. So the free window must be an activation program: zero-deal case-card wizard and fit-line wizard at launch, pitching guidance in onboarding, and kit-view → inquiry tracking from day one. The tracking data doubles as the month-6 sales pitch. Pricing reality: the market's only proven price anchor is ~$30/mo (Beacons Plus), with daily-fresh stats free — so $150/$300 is defensible only as a done-for-you service, never as hosting/stats, and stat freshness can never be paywalled.

**4.2 Booster menu rewritten.** Killed: media-kit PDF and press kit as products (free export button allowed), standalone testimonials (they live inside case cards), live social feeds, paid stat-freshness. Absorbed into free: curated samples (hard cap 3–6), contact forms. Reframed: booking calendar is a commodity — the sellable part is the response-time promise + terms wrapper; rate calculator never standalone — bundled into the rights menu. New top two (not in the original plan): **(1) case-study builder + post-campaign report generator** — flagship, wins re-bookings where the money consolidates; explicit fallback to free-tier acquisition hook if nobody buys it (decide the trigger metric in advance); **(2) rights & whitelisting menu + contract templates** — captures the measured +39.9% usage-rights premium; sell as deal velocity + money capture; needs a lawyer before shipping. Kit view/click analytics survives (~$30/mo anchor).

**4.3 Two functional presets, one buyer-first skeleton.** Influencer and UGC are different markets (different buyers, pricing, proof). One system, two presets differing in what's hidden: UGC hides audience demographics, leads with the work library, **prices ON by default**; Influencer leads with fit line + case cards, **dollar figures OFF by default** pending brand-side research. Template/Custom/The Block remain the aesthetic axis on top. Non-negotiables for every kit: server-rendered (loads without JavaScript — competitor Pillar's kits fail link previews; this is a marketable reliability point), mobile-first with key info in 2–3 phone screens, every stat dated, instrumented from day one.

**4.4 Fun stuff below the fold.** Fan wall / Top-8 stay as growth features but move off the brand-facing path — brands pass/fail in 5–10 seconds.

**4.5 Layer 4 demoted.** Kits are skipped exactly where repeat money consolidates (warm re-bookings — the post-campaign report is that artifact; TikTok Shop; marketplace UGC). Don't build a payments/escrow platform. Keep the light Stripe Connect cut but expect low volume; the flagship report booster is what keeps us in the re-booking money loop.

**4.6 Cost the homework.** "Every free kit costs near-zero" and "we do the homework per creator" can't both be fully true. The case-card homework (chasing a creator's past-collab data, writing it in buyer language, curating samples) is simultaneously the cost center and the moat — it's the exact work DIY-tool users never do, and it can't be fully automated without degrading to what DIY tools already output (and anything fully automatable is copyable — Beacons could ship proof-first defaults in a quarter). Measure minutes per kit, price the labor, derive the true cost per free kit, THEN set volume targets and the month-6 conversion rate needed to break even.

**4.7 Marketing hygiene.** A banned-numbers list exists (see 03-copy-bank): the "78% of brands request a media kit" stat is fabricated, all specific read-time stats are lore, vendor speed claims are quarantined. Never use them in any copy. Use the verified quote bank instead; every claim dated.

**What survives untouched or stronger:** the free wedge, all growth loops, The Block (out of research scope — survives as moat), Layer 3 builds (strengthened: done-for-you is the one moat a SaaS can't copy without becoming a service), the quiet-sell mechanic (now evidence-backed via brand-side pull). New positioning weapon: honest tier-routing — telling creators "at your tier you need receipts, not a kit" converts the kits-are-dead objection into credibility.
